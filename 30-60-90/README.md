# 30 · 60 · 90

What the first quarter looks like, sequenced by what unblocks revenue rather than by
what a framework's table of contents happens to list first.

Two constraints shape all of it. Midpage is **nine people**, so anything that costs an
engineer more than a few hours a week will not happen — every item below is designed to
be done *for* the team rather than *by* it. And the Series A has not closed (one Form D,
May 2025, nothing since), which means the calendar is set by whenever diligence starts,
not by an audit date.

---

## Days 1–30 — Answer the questions that are already being asked

**Reconcile the subprocessor lists.** Eighteen against fifteen. Establish which is
authoritative, whether advance notice went out for Pinecone, Datadog, and Intercom, and
publish one list. This is days, not weeks, and it closes a live contractual question.
*(F2)*

**Reconcile the retention claims.** Read the actual contract terms for all four model
providers, per endpoint, and write one paragraph that is true across every surface —
then put that paragraph on all of them. The zero-retention claim and the sixty-day claim
are probably both true of different things; nobody can currently tell. *(F1)*

**Fix the two-word compliance error and the two traffic figures.** One deploy each.
Free. *(F3, F5)*

**Establish what the Berlin office is.** Entity, headcount, function, and whether
processing decisions are taken there. Everything in the EU workstream waits on this
answer, and the answer takes one conversation. *(F4)*

**Build the questionnaire answer bank.** Not a tool — a document. The forty questions
that arrive most often, answered once, with the source surface cited for each. At nine
people this is the single highest-leverage artifact, because it converts a founder's
afternoon into a paralegal's ten minutes.

---

## Days 31–60 — Turn assertions into records

**The four evidence artifacts.** Subprocessor assessment register with a stated cadence;
notice log; per-provider contract extract mapping training prohibitions and retention
terms to endpoints; pipeline attestation for the no-training claim. These are the four
that convert the AI commitments from the weakest part of the public record into the
strongest. See [claims-to-evidence](../04-evidence-and-audit/claims-to-evidence.md).

**Model routing as a governed change type.** A day to design against the real pipeline,
minutes per change afterwards. It is the control that stops F1 and F2 recurring.
*(see [03](../03-secure-development/model-routing-change-management.md))*

**The EU position, written down.** Main establishment, lead supervisory authority,
Article 27 representative or a documented reason none is required, and the transfer
analysis re-run against whatever the answer turns out to be. Then publish it — the
publication is the control that stops this being a question in every future diligence
cycle. *(F4)*

**PHI flow map and the BAA chain.** Where medical records enter, which paths they take,
and whether a BAA exists at each hop. This determines whether the HIPAA attestation
covers what readers assume it covers.

**PCI scoping memo.** Probably three pages ending in SAQ A. It exists so the question
has a document attached instead of an explanation.

---

## Days 61–90 — Build the surface that sells

**One reconciled data-handling statement.** End to end, in language a litigator reads
before uploading a client file: Midpage storage, model-provider retention, the training
prohibition, the analytics boundary, deletion timeline. This is the artifact that
answers a third of every enterprise questionnaire and the one that belongs in the
product, not only on the site. *(F6, F7)*

**End-user documentation and a changelog.** Index the sixteen tutorials that already
exist, add a user-docs section, ship a changelog with a feed. Mostly routing, not
writing. *(F7)*

**AI governance scaffold, scoped to nine people.** NIST AI RMF as the working frame
because it is free and non-certifying; a model and system inventory; an ISO 42001
*roadmap* rather than an ISO 42001 project. Certification is a twelve-to-eighteen-month
programme and would be the wrong thing to start at this size.

**Series A diligence pack.** Assembled once, kept current: scope boundaries, the
evidence artifacts, the EU position, the subprocessor register, the reconciled claims.
When the round starts, this is the difference between two weeks of scramble and an email
with a link in it.

---

## What is deliberately not here

No ISO 27001 project. No penetration test procurement. No GRC platform migration —
Vanta is already in place and works. No policy-writing exercise: policies that nobody
operates are the most common thing a compliance hire produces in a first quarter and the
least useful.

Each of those becomes right later. None of them is what unblocks the next enterprise
contract or the next diligence request, which is the only test this plan is scored
against.
